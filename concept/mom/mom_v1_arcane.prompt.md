## The family resemblance — the thing to judge these on

The link is built from six things, and they are load-bearing in every sheet:

- **The grin.** The huge, wide, open, too-many-teeth grin is *hers first*. He got
  it from her. Same mouth in both refs: corners past the width of the eyes, big
  square front teeth, all gums and enamel.
- **The heavy lids.** His signature sleepy hooded eyes are her eyes, worn
  differently — on him they read dopey, on her they read *patient*, which is
  worse. **This is what actually carried the resemblance in the output.**
- **The build.** Thin, wiry, stringy, long-necked, narrow-shouldered, knobby.
- **The bones.** Long jaw, pointed chin, high hard cheekbones, small wedge nose.
- **The blond.** His is clean and pale; hers is the same blond fifteen years and
  several bottles later — darker, brassier, warmer.
- **The shape on top.** Her curly bob and his bowl cut are the same silhouette: a
  rounded mass framing the face, sitting above the ears, ends turning under and
  flicking out. Curly against straight is the only difference.

**The scold works because the grin is switched off.** Her default face is his
face; when she pulls it down into a flat level line, the kid knows exactly what
he has done. Sheet A is that face. Sheet B is the grin coming back at the wrong
moment. Sheet C spans between them.

## Sheet A — the scold (hero image)

Vertical 3:4, 2K. Refs: the boy (IMAGE 1), the mom photograph (IMAGE 2).

**Verbatim, as sent.** Note the paragraph beginning *"Three attempts have now

```
A character concept illustration of a woman in her mid-thirties. Vertical 3:4.

=== READ THIS FIRST — THE RENDERING IS THE WHOLE ASSIGNMENT ===

The first attached image (the blond boy) is not loose inspiration. It is the
TARGET. This new picture must look like the SAME ARTIST painted it, in the same
style, on the same afternoon, for the same film. Judge every decision below
against it.

Three things about that image are non-negotiable and all three were lost last
time:

1. VISIBLE CHUNKY BRUSHWORK. Every surface is hand-painted with thick loaded
   directional gouache strokes. You can count the strokes. Brush marks are left
   in and celebrated. ABSOLUTELY NO smooth digital gradients. NO airbrushed skin.
   NO soft blended shading. NO clean vector rendering. If a cheek or a sleeve
   looks smoothly blended, the image has failed.
2. EXTREME VALUE CONTRAST, ON HER. Genuinely deep, near-black shadow masses
   against hot luminous lit planes near white. Look at how dark that boy's shadow
   side goes and how hot his lit side goes. SHE MUST BE BOTH THE BRIGHTEST AND
   THE DARKEST THING IN THE PICTURE. The sun falls directly ON HER — she is not
   standing in shade next to a patch of light. Do NOT produce a soft, even,
   mid-range image, and do NOT solve the contrast by darkening the background
   while leaving her evenly lit. That is the exact failure to avoid.
3. HEAVY CARICATURE. That boy is not a realistic child. His proportions are
   pushed far past naturalism. This woman must be pushed EXACTLY AS FAR. She is
   a caricature, not a portrait. A handsome, naturalistic, well-proportioned
   woman is a failure.

=== REFERENCE ROLES ===

IMAGE 1 (the blond boy) — governs the RENDER completely: brushwork, palette,
value range, saturation, light, and the degree of caricature. He is also THIS
WOMAN'S SON. Do not copy his pose, wardrobe, age or setting.

IMAGE 2 (the photograph of the laughing woman in glasses) — governs her FACE and
her HAIR: bone structure, the long throat and prominent collarbones, the big
round wire-rimmed glasses, and the curly bob exactly as photographed. DISCARD
the photography, the lighting, the interior, the white top and her clothing
entirely.

=== THE FAMILY RESEMBLANCE — the point of the picture ===

She must read as unmistakably that boy's mother. Build her face out of his,
exaggerated the same amount:

- A LONG NARROW JAW tapering to a small pointed chin.
- HARD HIGH CHEEKBONES with clear hollows carved beneath them.
- A SMALL TRIANGULAR WEDGE of a nose — minimal, graphic, not a realistic nose.
- HEAVY HOODED DROOPY EYELIDS, low and half-lidded. His sleepy eyes are hers.
- A LONG SKINNY TENDONY NECK holding the head high.
- Her blond is his blond fifteen years later: darker, warmer, brassier.

Design her as the adult female version of that exact face. Not a generic woman
standing near him.

=== THE RENDER — painted 3D, the manner of Arcane ===

Real dimensional sculpted form, hand-painted all over. Form is modelled with
LIGHT: distinct planes of light and shadow, shape-shadows with crisp painted
edges, a hard terminator that breaks along brushstroke shapes instead of
blurring. Planes, not airbrush.

Stroke direction wraps the form — strokes sweeping around the mass of the hair,
running down the drape of a sleeve, cutting across the plane of a cheek. Where
two colours meet the brush edge stays visible: hand-made, slightly wavering,
never a clean cut. Individual strokes read clearly at full frame; a trouser leg
is a couple dozen visible strokes, not hundreds. Let loaded edges ridge where
they overlap and let dry patches scuff.

Matte throughout. No wet plastic shine, no glossy highlights on cloth or skin.
No ink outlines, no line art — all definition comes from painted shape, value
and edge.

=== VALUE AND COLOUR — high contrast, saturated ===

WIDE value range, deep darks, hot lights, nothing sitting safely in the middle.

- LIGHT is warm and saturated: golden and rust-orange, near-molten.
- SHADOW is deeply and openly COLOURED — never grey, never neutral, never just a
  darker version of the lit colour. Shadows drive hard into saturated deep teal
  and indigo. Coloured teal bounce kicks up into the shadow side of her face.
- The picture lives on that hot/cold tension.

PALETTE — hue anchors, taken to their SATURATED and DEEP extremes:
  paper-cream #f2e4cc   sand #d9c0a3        khaki #c2a878
  olive #8f7a3d         sky-teal #3fbdb3    terracotta #b0764a
  rust #c95f33          mall-mauve #a98794  coral #f0a082
  golden #eec078        warm charcoal #42403b (never pure black)

HER MOUTH IS THE HOTTEST NOTE IN THE PICTURE — coral-red lipstick, immaculate,
the single most saturated thing in the frame. The eye lands there first.

NO mint, no pale aqua-green anywhere — that colour is reserved for something
else in this film.

=== LIGHT — a hard raking key, NOT a blown-out backlight ===

SHE IS STANDING IN THE SUNBEAM. Low, hard afternoon sun rakes in from one side
at a steep angle and lands squarely on her body — not on the floor beside her.

It carves her into TWO CLEAR HALVES with one decisive value jump running straight
down her:
Three attempts have now come back with her sitting in an even, polite mid-tone
while only the background went dark. That is the failure. Push her own range far
harder than feels comfortable:

- THE LIT HALF is hot, near-white golden. Blazing luminous planes on the
  cheekbone, the brow, the top of the hair mass, one shoulder, the forearm, the
  raised hand and its painted nail, one leg. Push these almost to white — this is the brightest thing
  in the picture.
- THE SHADOW HALF goes deep, dark and saturated, nearly black in teal and
  indigo: under the hair, under the jaw, down her far side, beneath the
  shoulder-pad shelf, inside every fold of the blouse and the trouser pleats.
  This is the darkest thing in the picture.

That hard division IS the picture. A crisp painted terminator separates the two
halves, breaking along brushstroke shapes rather than blurring. A long
hard-edged diagonal shadow thrown from her feet across the floor toward camera.

Do not blow the background out to white. Do not light her only from behind. Do
not leave her in even, ambient, unlit shade.

=== THE WOMAN — build and proportion ===

Mid-thirties. THIN, wiry, stringy, hard-working. No softness and no glamour
anywhere. Caricatured hard, the same distance past naturalism as her son:

- Very long skinny neck, head sitting high on it.
- Narrow bony shoulders, thin arms, sharp elbows, long bony hands.
- Prominent collarbones, a long exposed throat, hollow above the collarbone.
- Big head for the body, carrying an even bigger hair mass.
- Long legs, very high waist, narrow through the hip. Angular everywhere.

She is TALL and reads that way — she owns the doorway.

=== THE HAIR — the curly bob ===

A late-80s / early-90s permed BOB, taken straight from the photograph. SHORT: it
stops at the jaw, at the very longest the chin. It never reaches the shoulders
and there is no length down her back. All that length has gone into VOLUME — the
mass is wide, round and high, half again the width of her head.

Springy curls throughout, dark honey blond going brassy and hot where the sun
hits. Soft and bouncy and lived-in, NOT lacquered, NOT teased into a stiff
helmet, not a beehive, not stiff big mall hair.

Paint it as ONE ROUNDED GRAPHIC MASS in value and silhouette — broad painted
planes of light and deep shadow describing its volume — with the curl read as
scalloped notches, bumps and flicked ends around the OUTER CONTOUR, plus a few
individual curls where the light cuts the edge and sets them glowing. The
interior stays broad and simple. Not a field of hundreds of separate ringlets.

Its silhouette deliberately rhymes with her son's bowl cut: the same rounded mass
framing the face, ends turning under and flicking out. Hers is curly, his is
straight, and that is meant to be the only difference.

=== THE GLASSES — MANDATORY, DO NOT OMIT ===

She wears large round wire-rimmed glasses. They were left out of the last
attempt; they are required. Thin warm-gold frames, big round lenses covering
her cheekbones, faintly amber-tinted glass, sitting exactly as they do in the
photograph.

Paint them as one bold graphic shape on the face. The hot key catches the top rim
as a hard bright arc. One lens flares partly opaque with reflected sky so a
single eye reads clearly through glass while the other becomes a flat saturated
teal plate. No fussy realistic glass. Small gold hoop earrings.

=== THE FACE — the expression ===

Features designed as bold graphic shapes, modelled with light. No blush, no rosy
cheeks, no freckles, no smooth photoreal skin.

She is the SWEETEST FACE IN THE COUNTY and right now that face is switched off.

NOT ANGRY. SERIOUS. Level, patient, unhurried, immovable. The mouth that is
normally split wide open in her son's enormous grin is pulled flat: lips closed,
corners level, one straight coral line. That flat line is the entire threat.
Eyes heavy and hooded, half-lidded, aimed straight down the barrel of her son —
not narrowed into a glare. Brows level, not angled down. Chin slightly lowered so
she looks out from under the lids.

She loves him, she has exactly one rule, and he has just stepped on it. Play it
completely straight — not a comic scold, not a harridan, not exasperated. The
temperature of a judge.

=== WARDROBE — 1988 mall-glam armour, worn in 1993 ===

- BLOUSE in mall-mauve #a98794 — a proper BUTTON-FRONT BLOUSE with a pointed
  collar, not a plain sweatshirt or a crewneck. Driven to a deep saturated
  plum-rose in the shadow planes and going hot pale pink-gold where the light
  rakes it. Do not render it as a flat even mid-tone lilac.
- THE SHOULDER PADS ARE STRUCTURAL AND MUST READ. Enormous 1988 pads pushing the
  shoulder line out into a HARD HORIZONTAL SHELF with a sharp square corner at
  each shoulder — wider than her hips, and after the hair the widest thing about
  her. The silhouette from the neck out should be a flat right-angled T. If the
  shoulders look soft, sloped or rounded, that is wrong. Full gathered sleeves
  below the shelf, cuffs buttoned narrow at the wrist.
- PANTS high-waisted and pleated at the waistband in sand #d9c0a3, going golden
  in the light and deep teal in the folds, tapered to the ankle. Thin belt.
- HANDS BARE. No gloves, no oven mitts, nothing on her hands at any point. Long
  slender bony fingers, prominent knuckles, hard well-kept nails filed to a neat
  oval and painted the SAME hot saturated red as her lipstick — the nails and the
  mouth are the only two places that colour appears on her, and they rhyme.

=== POSE ===

FULL BODY, FRAMED LOOSE. Her whole figure from the top of the hair to the SOLES
OF HER SHOES sits inside the frame, with clear empty floor visible beneath her —
leave a margin of at least a tenth of the picture height between her shoes and
the bottom edge. The last two attempts cropped her feet off; do not crop the
feet. She wears plain flat leather court shoes.

THREE-QUARTER VIEW, her body turned partly away from camera, not square-on and
symmetrical.

Standing squarely, weight even, absolutely still — she has stopped, and the
stopping is the point. Spine straight, chin down, shoulders level.

ONE BARE FINGER RAISED — the index finger of her bare hand extended straight up
out of a loose fist, lifted to about chest height in a single unhurried wag. Long,
slender and precise, with the painted nail on the end of it doing the pointing. It
must read unmistakably as a wagging finger. Her other hand rests on her hip. That
raised finger is the only moving thing in the picture, and the hot key catches it
and the nail.

The silhouette reads as a VILLAIN ENTRANCE played straight: hair, shoulder pads
and stance making one big, dark, monumental shape.

=== SETTING ===

The mouth of a 1993 garage, kept simple and dark so she dominates. Deep
saturated teal shadow through most of it, one hot golden slab of raking sunlight
across the concrete floor, and the vague dark suggestion of garage clutter at the
frame edges. No readable props, no scenery detail, no text on anything. The
background stays darker and lower-contrast than she is.

=== PERIOD ===
1993, painted deadpan by a 1962 poster artist. The clothes are 1988. The painting
never acknowledges either.

=== AVOID ===
smooth digital gradients, airbrushed skin, soft blended shading, clean vector
rendering, low contrast, even mid-range values, pastel washes, washed-out colour,
grey or neutral shadows, blown-out white backgrounds, ink outlines, line art,
plastic CG shine, photorealism, depth-of-field blur, rosy cheeks or blush,
glamour-model beauty, fashion-illustration elegance, a naturalistic or
well-proportioned or curvy figure, missing glasses, lacquered helmet hair,
beehive, teased stiff big-hair, hair past the shoulders, long hair, straight
hair, angry or shouting or shrewish expression, symmetrical front-on pose,
cropped feet, hands on both hips, superhero posing, cactus or desert or
mid-century furniture, mint or aqua-green, text, labels or watermarks.
```

### Edit pass A2 — what actually finished the image

The round above produced everything except the finger wag and the shoulder pads.
Rather than re-roll and risk the light, the image was passed back in as the only
ref with this edit prompt. **This two-stage approach worked every time it was
used and is the recommended way to fix a near-miss here.**

```
Edit the attached illustration (IMAGE 1). Keep EVERYTHING about it identical —
the same character and face, the same hooded eyes and glasses, the same curly
bob, the same lighting with its hot near-white lit half and deep saturated teal
shadow half, the same palette, the same brushwork and visible strokes, the same
pose and stance, the same full-body framing with the floor and her shoes
showing, the same background.

Change exactly three things:

1. THE RAISED HAND BECOMES A WAGGING FINGER. Right now it is an open palm.
   Repaint it as a loose bare fist with ONE index finger extended straight up out
   of it, in a single unhurried scolding wag — long, slender, precise, the painted
   nail on the end of it doing the pointing. It must read unmistakably as a
   wagging finger. Keep the same hand position and height and the same hot key
   light on it and on the nail.

2. HARD 1988 SHOULDER PADS. Rebuild the shoulder line into a sharp horizontal
   SHELF with a square right-angled corner at each shoulder, clearly wider than
   her hips — after the hair, the widest thing about her. The silhouette from
   neck to shoulder should be a flat right angle, not a soft slope. Keep the
   same blouse colour and the same sleeves below the shelf.

3. A POINTED COLLAR on the blouse instead of the round band neckline, sitting
   open at the throat.

Change nothing else. Do not restyle, do not relight, do not recolour, do not
reframe, do not smooth the brushwork.
```

### What it did without being asked

- **Sheet C came back with no captions.** The boy's shoe and expression sheets
  could not be talked out of labelling cells across multiple attempts; this one
  obeyed first time and again on the re-roll. Do not assume that holds — check
  before assuming no cleanup is needed.
- **Sheet C dropped the shoulders and blouse**, returning heads on necks against
  the flat field. Asked for "head and shoulders, cut off at the chest". The
  result is arguably better for a face board and was kept.
- Sheet A put an old fridge in the garage. Unprompted, period-correct, kept.

### Known deviations, live

- **Contrast never fully reached the boy's range.** Four rounds of escalating
  language got Sheet A most of the way — a real lit-half/shadow-half carve — but
  Sheet B and Sheet C both settled somewhat flatter than
  `boy_v3_arcane_b-backyard-dusk.png`. If this matters, the lever that worked was
  the two-stage edit pass, not stronger wording in the first prompt.
- **Sheet B's wrecked backyard mostly vanished** in the sky edit — the smoke,
  debris and fire of the round before it were replaced by a plain field. Simpler
  and more poster-like, so it was kept, but it is no longer legibly the
  post-battle yard the script describes.
- Sheet B's pose is square-on rather than the three-quarter that was asked for.
- Hair reads a touch lighter and more golden on Sheet C than on Sheet A.

### Reproducing these

Every call was made with a small generator that reads `GEMINI_API_KEY` from
`.env`, attaches labelled refs inline, and writes a `.request.json` sidecar next
to each image recording model, aspect, size, prompt file and ref list. It lives
in this session's scratchpad rather than `tools/` — **nothing in `tools/` reads
`concept/`, and that boundary is deliberate.** If concept generation becomes
routine it is worth promoting; until then the prompts above are the record and
any client that can attach images will reproduce them.
