## Two departures from `docs/treatment/style.md`, both deliberate

1. **These sheets carry outlines; the film's faces do not.** § Characters says
   "No linework on a face." That is a rule about final frames. `boy_modelsheet`
   already draws the face with clean outlines because a reference plate has to
   be unambiguous, and these match it. Do not read the outlines as a look
   decision.
2. **The eyes act.** § Characters says "the eyes stay sleepy and the mouth does
   all the acting." Treated as **guidance, not a rule** (Ian, 2026-08-06) — the
   hooded lids are his resting state and most cells keep them, but the peaks
   (the box reveal, the oh-no, the blast) open the eyes. If that turns out to be
   wrong the fix is cheap: it is one clause in each cell description.

Flat and shadowless like the rest of the boy's modelling references — no
tilt-dab, no atmosphere. Same allowed departure `boy_modelsheet.prompt.md` takes.

## Sheet 1 — the getting (sq010–sq020)

```
A character EXPRESSION SHEET: six head-and-shoulders studies of the SAME boy,
matching the attached character reference exactly. Landscape, roughly 3:2.

=== WHAT THIS IS FOR ===
This is a technical reference for building and animating a 3D face, not an
illustration. It will be read side by side to see how one face changes across a
film. Clarity and consistency matter more than mood, atmosphere or beauty.

=== LAYOUT ===
Exactly SIX cells in a clean 3-across, 2-down arrangement. One head per cell,
large, centred, with generous empty space around it. The cells are separated by
EMPTY BACKGROUND ONLY — no dividing lines, no rules, no boxes, no panel borders,
no frames. Do not draw a grid.
Head and shoulders only, cut off at the chest.

=== CRITICAL: THE SAME HEAD SIX TIMES ===
This is one character drawn six times, not six characters. In every cell the
head is the SAME SIZE, at the SAME distance, from the SAME dead-on FRONT angle,
level with the camera, facing straight forward. The skull, the jaw, the hairline,
the bowl cut, the ears, the nose and the neck are IDENTICAL in all six. Only the
eyes, brows and mouth change. If the face structure drifts between cells the
sheet is useless.

=== THE CHARACTER — identical in all six ===
An eleven-year-old boy, taken exactly from the attached reference. Thick blond
bowl cut as ONE solid helmet-shaped mass with a straight fringe cut square across
the brow, a small notch parting at its centre, the ends curling under just above
the ears — no strands, no locks, no wisps. Heavy droopy hooded eyelids with a
distinctly DARK shadowed lid area above small eyes. Thin high brows. Small simple
wedge nose. Large simple ears. Two oversized square front teeth. Large head, long
thin neck, narrow shoulders, oversized boxy black t-shirt. Leave the t-shirt
graphic blank.

=== THE SIX ===
1. NEUTRAL REST. Mouth closed and relaxed, lips together in a soft even line,
   corners level. Lids heavy and half closed, brows level and unbothered. Doing
   nothing and thinking nothing. This is the base the other five depart from.
2. THE DEVIOUS GRIN. A closed-lipped grin pulled hard to ONE side, that corner
   climbing much higher than the other, cheek bunched under it. Lids dropped
   low, brows pushed down and in toward the nose. He is not showing this to
   anybody — it is entirely private and he is enjoying it enormously.
3. WONDER OVER THE RIM. He has just seen inside the box. The jaw hangs slack and
   the mouth falls open in a soft round O — no teeth bared, just open. For once
   the heavy lids LIFT and the eyes go round and bright. Brows high. Completely
   undefended.
4. THE SHOVE. Pure effort. Lips pulled wide back off the teeth so both rows are
   bared and clamped together, jaw driving forward, cheeks tight. Eyes squeezed
   down to slits, brows driven hard down. Ugly and committed.
5. DOING ARITHMETIC. The eyes cut HARD to one side, pupils jammed into the
   corners, while the head stays facing forward. The mouth pushes small and
   tight and slides toward the same side. One brow climbs, the other stays down.
   He heard the instruction. He is working out the loophole.
6. THE BEAD. Sighting down something. ONE eye squeezed fully shut, the other
   narrowed hard to a slot, that cheek pushed up. Mouth a small tight line
   dragged off to one side. Brows down. Total concentration.

=== LIGHTING — FLAT AND EVEN ===
Completely flat, even, shadowless lighting, identical in all six cells. NO key
light, NO rim light, NO coloured light, NO cast shadow, NO shadow under the chin
or the fringe, no ambient occlusion, no atmosphere. The dark hooded lid area is
the character's own colouring, NOT a shadow — keep it in even flat lighting.

=== RENDERING ===
Flat local colours matching the attached reference: flat blond hair, flat even
skin, flat dark grey-black tee, white teeth. Clean confident outlines around the
head and the features so every shape reads unambiguously. Minimal internal
detail — no painterly brushwork, no texture, no grain, no hatching, no rendered
cheekbones, no blush, no freckles, no skin shading.

=== BACKGROUND ===
Completely plain, flat, light neutral grey. Empty. No scenery, no props, no
gradient, no vignette, no border, no drop shadow.

=== AVOID ===
different characters between cells, drifting skull or jaw shape, drifting hair
shape, drifting hair length, individual hair strands, different head sizes
between cells, three-quarter or profile angles, tilted heads, turned heads,
perspective distortion, blush, rosy cheeks, freckles, dramatic lighting, rim
light, cast shadows, coloured light, scenery, painterly brushwork, heavy texture,
photorealism, text, labels, captions, numbers, arrows, grids, panel borders,
dividing lines between cells, watermarks, signatures.
```

## Sheet 2 — caught, and got (sq020–sq070)

Identical prompt to Sheet 1 with `=== THE SIX ===` replaced by the block below.
Everything else — layout, identity, lighting, rendering, background, avoid — is
word for word the same, which is the point: the two sheets have to look like one
shoot.

```
=== THE SIX ===
1. PEW. Absorbed in play, making the gunfire noise himself. The mouth pushes
   FORWARD into a small tight round O, lips pursed hard. Eyes narrowed but
   bright and alive, brows up. Utterly committed to the bit.
2. CAUGHT. A shadow has just fallen across him and he has frozen mid-aim. The
   mouth is still stuck in the small round O of the previous cell — it has not
   caught up yet — but the eyes have gone WIDE and flat and the brows have shot
   up. The whole face has stopped dead. All the blood has left it.
3. TOLD OFF. Receiving one sharp instruction from an adult he actually respects.
   Mouth a small flat closed line, corners level, chin tucked down slightly so
   he is looking UP from under the fringe. Lids heavy, eyes steady and aimed
   straight at camera. Not sulking, not smirking — listening, and taking it
   seriously.
4. SAVORING. Squaring up to something he has been waiting all day for. The big
   open toothy grin returning, but SLOWLY — wide, unhurried, both oversized
   front teeth showing, lids dropped low and lazy over it, chin lifted, brows
   level. Relish, not excitement.
5. OH NO. The mouth drops fully open into a wide flat rectangle of dread, teeth
   apart, corners pulled down and back. The eyes go as WIDE as they will go,
   round and staring. Brows lift and drive together in the middle. Every part of
   the face is retreating.
6. BLAST-DAZED. Flat on his back with the wind knocked out of him. Everything
   slack: the mouth hangs open loose and slightly crooked, the lids droop to
   half open over unfocused eyes, brows limp. The fringe is knocked out of line
   and there are a few soot smudges. Nobody is home.
```

## Sheet 3 — motion, and two utilities

Same prompt again, this `=== THE SIX ===`. Cells 5 and 6 are not story beats —
they are the ends of the range, which a face rig needs and a film never shows.

```
=== THE SIX ===
1. IMPATIENT GLEE. He cannot keep still. The huge open toothy grin at its full
   width, corners pushed past the width of the eyes, both oversized front teeth
   and the full row showing. Eyes squeezed up into happy crescents, brows high,
   cheeks shoved up into the lids. The whole face pushed upward.
2. MID-DIVE PANIC. Running flat out with things going off around him. The mouth
   stretches wide open into a flat terrified rectangle with the teeth showing,
   corners hauled back. Eyes wide and hard, brows up. Head thrown slightly back.
   Terror moving at speed.
3. THE LOOK. One flat sideways look at somebody he is in trouble with, exchanged
   without moving his head. Eyes cut hard to one side, mouth a tight closed
   line, brows level, chin slightly down. Completely deadpan. Two people who
   both know exactly what is about to happen.
4. SPRINT WHOOP. Running for his life and loving it. Mouth wide open in a shout,
   jaw dropped, teeth showing, corners up not down. Eyes squeezed shut with the
   effort, brows up. Terror and delight at the same time.
5. BLINK. Eyes fully CLOSED — both lids down, drawn as two calm curved lines,
   lashes not required. Brows level and relaxed. The mouth holds his default
   closed easy line. Nothing else in the face is doing anything. This is a
   utility pose, not an emotion.
6. JAW WIDE. The mouth open as wide as it will physically go, jaw dropped to its
   limit, both rows of teeth apart with the dark interior of the mouth visible
   between them, lips stretched round. Eyes and brows perfectly neutral and
   relaxed. The extreme end of the mouth's travel, nothing more.
```

## Sheet 4 — mouth chart

Nine crops of the lower face. This is the sheet that earns its keep: the boy's
mouth carries most of his acting, so this is the shape vocabulary the face gets
built from.

```

the attached character reference exactly. Landscape, roughly 3:2.

=== WHAT THIS IS FOR ===
This is a technical reference for building a 3D face, not an illustration. It
isolates one character's mouth shapes so they can be modelled as a set.

=== LAYOUT ===
Exactly NINE cells in a clean 3-across, 3-down arrangement. One mouth per cell,
large, centred, with generous empty space around it. The cells are separated by
EMPTY BACKGROUND ONLY — no dividing lines, no rules, no boxes, no panel borders,
no frames, no grid.
Each cell is a CROP of the lower face: from just below the eyes down to just
below the chin. No eyes, no brows, no hair, no ears — the bottom of the nose,
the mouth, the cheeks either side of it, and the chin. The crop is identical in
every cell.

=== CRITICAL: THE SAME MOUTH NINE TIMES ===
One character drawn nine times. Same crop, same scale, same dead-on FRONT angle,
same lighting in every cell. The nose, the cheeks, the chin and the jaw width are
IDENTICAL throughout — only the mouth shape changes. Two oversized square front
teeth wherever teeth show.

=== THE NINE ===
Top row, left to right:
1. CLOSED NEUTRAL — lips together, corners level, a soft even relaxed line.
2. THE BIG GRIN — his signature. Open wide, corners pushed up and out past the
   width of the nose, the full row of teeth showing with the two oversized front
   teeth dominating.
3. DEVIOUS — closed lips pulled hard to one side, that corner climbing high,
   cheek bunched above it, the other corner staying level.

Middle row, left to right:
4. SMALL O — lips pursed forward into a tight little round hole, cheeks drawn
   in. Making a noise.
5. SHOUT — a big open rounded O, jaw well down, teeth showing at the top edge,

6. GRITTED — lips hauled back off the teeth so both rows are bared and clamped

Bottom row, left to right:
7. FLAT LINE — closed, corners dead level, a straight hard horizontal. Neither
   happy nor sad. Receiving bad news.
8. DREAD — dropped open into a wide flat rectangle, teeth apart, corners pulled
   down and back.
9. SLACK — hanging open loose and slightly crooked, jaw limp, lips soft, no

=== LIGHTING — FLAT AND EVEN ===
Completely flat, even, shadowless lighting, identical in all nine cells. NO key

=== RENDERING ===

=== BACKGROUND ===
Completely plain, flat, light neutral grey. Empty. No gradient, no vignette, no
border, no drop shadow.

=== AVOID ===
eyes, brows, hair, ears, whole heads, different characters between cells,
drifting nose or chin shape, different scale between cells, three-quarter or

dramatic lighting, cast shadows, coloured light, painterly brushwork, heavy
texture, photorealism, text, labels, captions, numbers, arrows, grids, panel
borders, dividing lines between cells, watermarks, signatures.
```

## What came back

One round. Much cleaner than the hand sheets, for a reason worth remembering:
**the identity block did the work.** Naming the bowl cut's centre notch, the dark
hooded lid area and the two oversized front teeth as explicit, checkable features
held the face steady across eighteen cells with no drift in skull, jaw or hair.

### What is right

- Identity holds across all three expression sheets. Same boy every cell.
- Every beat reads and every beat is distinct — `caught` in particular does the
  thing it was asked to do, keeping the mouth stuck in the previous cell's
  `pew` O while the eyes have already gone wide. That is the shot.
- `savoring` and `impatient glee` both produce the signature two oversized front
  teeth without being told twice.
- The mouth chart came back clean, unlabelled, with all nine shapes distinct.

### What had to be fixed after the fact

- **Sheets 1 and 2 captioned every cell; sheet 3 did not.** Same prompt, same
  call, different behaviour — so this is a coin flip, not something a wording can
  fix (the shoe sheet in `boy_hands_feet.prompt.md` lost the same argument three
  times). An inconsistent set is worse than either option, so the captions were
  **painted out** of sheets 1 and 2 to match sheet 3 and to match the prompt as
  written. The shot mapping above replaces them.
- The stripper needs a blank source band at least as tall as the patch. Sheet 2's
  heads run to within 90 px of the top edge, and the first attempt copied the top
  of a head into the gap under each cell. It now measures the tallest ink-free
  band and falls back to a flat fill of the sampled background when no band is
  tall enough.

### Known deviations, live

- **Brows read heavier than canon** in several cells — the reference has thin
  high brows and the sheets thicken them, most visibly in `doing arithmetic`.
- **Dentition is inconsistent.** `savoring`, `impatient glee` and mouth-chart
  cells 2 and 8 give the two oversized front teeth; `the shove` and mouth-chart
  cell 6 draw a full even row of small ones. The two big teeth are canon — take
  those cells with a pinch.
- The hair carries faint strand lines the identity block asks it not to. Harmless
  at this scale; it is one solid mass in silhouette, which is what the style rule
  is actually protecting.
- `jaw wide` (sheet 3, cell 6) drifts toward a second `blast-dazed` — its eyes
  should be neutral and are droopy. Usable as the mouth extreme, not as a face.

## In Blender

**Where 26.6 cm comes from.** Measured, not assumed: the bowl cut in
`boy_modelsheet_front.png` is 258 px wide at the sheet's own 1.0313 mm/px. On a
1.45 m figure that is a head nearly twice the width of a real eleven-year-old's —
the same deliberate caricature as the 27 cm shoe. The sculpt should match the
drawing, not anatomy. The three sheets each carry their own display size because
the generated head width drifted between them (939 / 1004 / 1030 px), so a single
scale would have made them disagree in world space.

**The mouth chart is enlarged on purpose.** Sized to life it would be a 36 cm
plate and unreadable. It is set to the width of the other three so the board
reads as one object; its cells are not measurable.
