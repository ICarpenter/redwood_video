**Logline:** A kid in the heartland 3D-prints a machine gun and some
human-sized action figures to shoot, and by sundown has accidentally
dragged his mom and the county sheriff into a three-way backyard war —
resolved, briefly, until the one thing Mom actually cared about tips over.

**Tone & look:** Claymation with a slightly more detailed look. Bright
colors. White trash with a bit of Americana. Spike & Mike animation
festival energy — cartoon violence played sunny, gags with wind-up and
payoff, nobody actually gets hurt except the scenery.

**Rule of the world:** guns are toys, consequences are cosmetic, and the
only sacred object in the entire film is a vintage Santa Claus lawn
decoration.

## The story (author's draft, near-verbatim)

Pedal steel yawns as the sun rises above a rural house. A young boy pushes
open the screen door as a delivery vehicle pulls away; a large package has
been left. He unboxes a large 3D printer in his garage — close-up on his
devious grin. The printer cartoonishly shits out a large claymation machine
gun with all the bells and whistles. The boy swings it around, drawing a
bead on things around the garage and yard. The shadow of Mom falls over the
scene like a villain's. He looks at her sheepishly as she wags her finger:
he'd better not shoot up that vintage Santa Claus decoration or anything
else she cares about.

The printer prints a human-sized action figure with a comical, nostalgic
visage. In the backyard, several of these figures stand posed as a firing
squad. The boy squares up and absolutely obliterates them — claymation
parts and pieces everywhere. Mid-barrage, Mom looks out the kitchen window
and admires her son's thoroughness (while ignoring his poor accuracy).

Out front, a rural sheriff rolls down the road in an old cruiser, admiring
the day with a contented, lazy smile. A stray ricochet blasts out his back
tire; the cruiser spins out into the drainage ditch. The boy keeps
shredding — features exploding like balloons filled with molten Play-Doh.
The sheriff struggles out of the wreck, has a very brief war flashback,
draws a comically large gun, and goes into a determined army crawl, salty
marine's mug and visible butt crack. He creeps down the side of the house,
passing a window — where Mom sees a disheveled armed man skulking. She
pulls her own rosco and starts unloading just as the sheriff rounds the
corner firing wildly, surprised the shots now come from two directions.

Full three-way chaos, everyone firing at whatever moves, until the boy's
spray finds the propane BBQ: mushroom cloud, everyone blasted off their
feet. The camera hovers over the partially charred backyard.

## Ending (CONFIRMED — "Not the Santa")

The smoke drifts. The three of them lie dazed in the char, coughing little
clay puffs. Slow, tentative aftermath: Mom and the sheriff recognize each
other (a name, a mangled hat held up in apology); sheepish nods all round;
for one warm beat it looks like this ends in sweet tea. Then — behind them
— the vintage Santa, charred black on one side, wobbles… and slowly tips
over dead.

Mom's face goes final boss. Final chorus: now SHE'S got the guns — the boy
and the sheriff exchange one look and RUN. The last image is the two of
them sprinting down the dirt road under heavy fire from Mom on the porch —
and the song dies dead on the final chorus hit (bar 111). Smash to black.
Title card rings out over the audio tail.

Why it works: pays off the planted gag, gives the final chorus a reason to
exist (the hook changes owners), and the abrupt cut IS the joke — Spike &
Mike endings don't linger.
